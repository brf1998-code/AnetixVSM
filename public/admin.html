How to read these numbers

Counts are for the window; open items, what is waiting, and the quiet stretch are as of now. Same shift means feedback to floor within 8 hours. To floor runs from the feedback item to the deploy (the oldest item in a batch). Manager wait is proposal drafted to decided, then ready to deployed. Stops are where builds stopped: the platform's own checks (gate), the reviewer, the tests, or something else. Quiet is days since the last reviewer or test stop, gate stop, and rollback; a long quiet stretch is the signal to try a cheaper build model. Edited is how many approved proposals the manager changed first (on the record since Sept 19). Decision times before the record use the run's start.

Ask Anetix the assistant on the board, managers only (docs/ASSISTANT.md). A demo's rows are what demo users typed into a panel that gave set answers.

Access pins

anetix.ai is not open to the public: a person gets in with a pin made here for their email and handed to them. They enter the pin with that email, name their company and pick a password, set up an authenticator app, and land on a demo board of their own with $10 of AI credit from us, about one module and three rounds of changes. When it runs out they add more on anetix.ai through Stripe ($30 buys $10). A build that is already running when the credit runs out still finishes; the overrun is written off at their next refill. A pin works only with its email, makes one company unless you say more, and expires. Cancel one and it stops working at once; the company it already made stays.

anetix.ai as a person with a pin sees it, in a new tab. A preview cannot make a company; to try the payment, make yourself a pin.

Backup and restore

The backup is one JSON file with every platform table (companies, module versions and their files, feedback, runs, docs, reviews, diagrams) and every company's live module data, plus the admin accounts (passwords and recovery codes as hashes, authenticator secrets encrypted). The sign-in log is left out. Railway keeps daily backups of the database too; take one of these before a restore or a big change. Restore replaces everything on the instance with the file and restarts the platform; it asks for a fresh code from your authenticator, and every admin signs in again afterwards.
